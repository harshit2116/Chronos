Field guide

How the world advances, and what each agent can know.

One turn, in order

  1. Scheduled incidents damage affected subsystems.
  2. Degraded sources propagate faults along dependencies.
  3. Each agent receives its own health, protection, incidents, and peer messages.
  4. Agents choose simultaneously. Affordable actions execute in rotating role order.
  5. Messages are queued for the next turn; the full trace is saved.

Any subsystem reaching zero ends the mission before agents act. Survive 12 turns to complete a run. Preserving service and credits distinguishes stronger outcomes.

Information boundaries

The console is an observer view. Agents do not receive the complete world state, incident schedule, or other agents’ private observations. Their only source of peer status is communication.

Model agents retain four recent decisions and receive the outcome of their last action. The inspector shows the observation supplied for each decision and a brief operational justification.

These are trusted, in-process agents in a fictional simulation. Observation filtering is not a security sandbox, and the scenarios are not calibrated models of operational risk.

Response playbook

Emergency actions use role-specific names. Their recovery and wear mechanics are shared across the four scenarios.
